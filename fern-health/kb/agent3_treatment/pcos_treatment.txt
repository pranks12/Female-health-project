POLYCYSTIC OVARY SYNDROME (PCOS) - TREATMENT GUIDE

Overview
--------
PCOS is one of the most common hormonal disorders affecting women of reproductive age,
estimated to affect 8–13% of women globally. It is characterized by a combination of
hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound.

Diagnostic Criteria (Rotterdam Criteria — 2 of 3 required)
1. Oligo- or anovulation
2. Clinical and/or biochemical hyperandrogenism
3. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (≥12 follicles per ovary or ovarian volume >10 mL)

Treatment by Goal

A. Menstrual Regulation
   - Combined oral contraceptives (COCs): First-line for cycle regulation and
     anti-androgen effect. Prefer progestins with low androgenic activity
     (e.g., drospirenone, cyproterone acetate where available).
   - Progestin-only withdrawal bleeds: Medroxyprogesterone acetate 10 mg for
     10 days every 1–3 months if COCs are contraindicated.

B. Hyperandrogenism (Acne, Hirsutism, Hair Loss)
   - COCs as above.
   - Spironolactone (100–200 mg/day): Anti-androgen; use with contraception.
   - Eflornithine cream: Slows facial hair growth.
   - Finasteride: For female pattern hair loss (use with contraception).
   - Topical retinoids and antibiotics for acne.

C. Ovulation Induction (Fertility)
   - Letrozole (2.5–7.5 mg, days 3–7): First-line ovulation induction; superior
     to clomiphene in PCOS.
   - Clomiphene citrate (50–150 mg, days 3–7): Alternative first-line agent.
   - Metformin: May restore ovulation, especially in insulin-resistant women;
     often combined with clomiphene.
   - Gonadotrophins: Second-line if oral agents fail; requires monitoring.
   - Laparoscopic ovarian drilling: Surgical option reducing androgen production.
   - IVF: For cases not responding to other treatments.

D. Metabolic Management (Insulin Resistance, Weight)
   - Lifestyle modification: FIRST AND MOST IMPORTANT intervention. Even 5–10%
     weight loss improves menstrual regularity, fertility, and metabolic markers.
     150+ minutes/week moderate exercise; low-glycemic-index diet.
   - Metformin (500–2000 mg/day): Improves insulin sensitivity; reduces androgen
     levels; supports weight management. Not a weight-loss drug per se.
   - Inositol (myo-inositol 2–4 g/day): Emerging evidence for improving
     insulin sensitivity and ovulation; generally safe.

E. Long-Term Health Risks and Prevention
   - Increased risk of type 2 diabetes: Screen regularly; lifestyle first-line.
   - Cardiovascular risk: Monitor lipids, blood pressure; encourage exercise.
   - Endometrial hyperplasia/cancer: Regular menstrual regulation essential to
     prevent unopposed estrogen effect on endometrium.
   - Mental health: Higher rates of anxiety and depression; screen and refer.
   - Sleep apnea: Screen, especially in overweight women with PCOS.

Monitoring
- Annual fasting glucose or HbA1c
- Lipid panel
- Blood pressure
- Body weight and BMI
- Menstrual calendar
