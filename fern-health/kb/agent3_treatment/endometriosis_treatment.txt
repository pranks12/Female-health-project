ENDOMETRIOSIS - TREATMENT GUIDE

Overview
--------
Endometriosis is a chronic, estrogen-dependent inflammatory condition where
endometrial-like tissue grows outside the uterus, most commonly on the ovaries,
fallopian tubes, and pelvic peritoneum. It affects approximately 10% of reproductive-age
women and is a leading cause of pelvic pain and infertility.

Symptoms
- Dysmenorrhea (often severe, worsening over time)
- Chronic pelvic pain (not only menstrual)
- Dyspareunia (painful intercourse)
- Dyschezia (painful bowel movements)
- Dysuria (painful urination)
- Infertility
- Fatigue

Treatment Approaches

A. Pain Management (Medical — Hormonal)
   First-Line:
   - Combined oral contraceptives (COCs): Continuous or cyclic use; suppresses
     menstruation and endometrial growth.
   - Progestins: Norethisterone, medroxyprogesterone acetate, or dienogest.
     Dienogest 2 mg daily is highly effective and well tolerated.
   - Levonorgestrel IUD (Mirena): Reduces dysmenorrhea significantly.

   Second-Line (GnRH Analogues / Antagonists):
   - GnRH agonists (e.g., leuprolide): Induce medical menopause; highly effective
     but significant side effects (bone loss, hot flashes); use with add-back
     hormone therapy (low-dose estrogen + progestin) to mitigate.
   - GnRH antagonists (e.g., elagolix): Oral; dose-dependent suppression; allows
     partial estrogen activity. Approved specifically for endometriosis.
   - Maximum 6-month treatment without add-back; 12 months with add-back.

   Analgesics:
   - NSAIDs: Essential adjunct for pain flares; take regularly rather than PRN
     for best effect during menstruation.
   - Low-dose naltrexone: Emerging adjunct for central sensitization.

B. Surgical Treatment
   - Laparoscopy: Gold standard for diagnosis AND treatment; excision or ablation
     of lesions. Excision generally preferred over ablation for deeper disease.
   - Endometrioma (ovarian cyst): Cystectomy preferred over drainage/ablation;
     associated with improved pain and fertility outcomes.
   - Hysterectomy ± bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy: For severe, refractory disease
     in women who have completed childbearing; not a guaranteed cure.
   - Bowel/bladder/ureteral surgery: Required for deep infiltrating endometriosis
     involving adjacent organs; specialist multidisciplinary approach.

C. Fertility Treatment
   - Surgical removal of endometriosis improves natural conception rates,
     especially for mild-moderate disease.
   - IVF: Effective for endometriosis-associated infertility; outcomes comparable
     to tubal factor infertility for stage I/II; slightly reduced for stage III/IV.
   - Ovarian reserve monitoring (AMH, antral follicle count): Important as
     endometriomas and surgery can reduce ovarian reserve.
   - Avoid repeat surgery on endometriomas before IVF if possible; risk to
     ovarian reserve outweighs benefit in most cases.

D. Non-Pharmacological / Complementary
   - Pelvic physiotherapy: Addresses pelvic floor dysfunction common in
     endometriosis; reduces dyspareunia.
   - Psychology/CBT: Central sensitization and pain catastrophizing are common;
     psychological support improves outcomes.
   - Diet: Anti-inflammatory diet (Mediterranean); some evidence for omega-3 and
     reduced red meat.
   - Exercise: Regular aerobic exercise reduces pain and inflammation.

Long-Term Management
- Endometriosis is a chronic condition requiring long-term management strategy.
- Recurrence after surgery is common without hormonal suppression.
- Post-surgical hormonal therapy recommended to delay recurrence.
- Regular review and individualized care essential.
- Multidisciplinary care (gynecology, pain management, physiotherapy, psychology).
