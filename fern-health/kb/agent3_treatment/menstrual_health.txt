MENSTRUAL HEALTH - TREATMENT GUIDE

Overview
--------
The menstrual cycle is a natural monthly process involving hormonal changes that prepare the body for possible pregnancy. A typical cycle is 21–35 days. Variations in length, flow, and symptoms can indicate underlying conditions that benefit from treatment.

Common Conditions and Treatments

1. Dysmenorrhea (Painful Periods)
   - Primary dysmenorrhea: No underlying disease; caused by prostaglandin release.
     Treatment: NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) taken 1–2 days before onset; hormonal
     contraceptives (pill, patch, ring) to reduce prostaglandin levels; heat therapy;
     regular aerobic exercise.
   - Secondary dysmenorrhea: Caused by conditions like endometriosis or fibroids.
     Treatment: Treat the underlying condition; hormonal therapy; surgical options
     if conservative treatment fails.

2. Menorrhagia (Heavy Periods)
   - Defined as blood loss >80 mL per cycle or periods lasting >7 days.
   - Treatment: NSAIDs to reduce blood loss; tranexamic acid; hormonal IUDs
     (levonorgestrel-releasing, e.g., Mirena); combined oral contraceptives;
     endometrial ablation for women not seeking future pregnancy; hysterectomy
     as a last resort.

3. Amenorrhea (Absent Periods)
   - Primary: No period by age 15. Evaluate for anatomical or chromosomal causes.
   - Secondary: Previously regular cycles stop for ≥3 months.
   - Treatment: Address underlying cause (stress, low body weight, thyroid disorders,
     PCOS, hyperprolactinemia). Hormone replacement therapy if indicated.

4. Premenstrual Syndrome (PMS) and PMDD
   - Symptoms: Mood swings, bloating, breast tenderness, fatigue, irritability.
   - PMDD: Severe form with significant functional impairment.
   - Treatment: SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline) — first-line for PMDD, especially
     luteal-phase dosing; combined oral contraceptives (drospirenone-containing);
     calcium supplementation (1200 mg/day); lifestyle modifications (exercise,
     sleep hygiene, reduced caffeine and alcohol).

Lifestyle Recommendations
- Track cycle with an app or diary for pattern recognition.
- Maintain a balanced diet rich in iron, magnesium, and omega-3 fatty acids.
- Regular moderate exercise reduces cramp severity and mood symptoms.
- Adequate hydration reduces bloating.
- Limit sodium intake in the luteal phase to reduce fluid retention.

When to Seek Medical Attention
- Periods suddenly becoming much heavier or more painful than usual.
- Bleeding between periods or after menopause.
- Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days.
- Symptoms significantly impacting daily functioning.
